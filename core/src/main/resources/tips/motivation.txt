# WRITING RULE FOR THIS POOL: this pool pushes. Short sentences, verbs up front, an actual
# imperative where it fits. Permission-giving lines ("it's fine to rest", "not every day has
# to be productive") are warm but they are not motivating, and they belong in wellbeing.txt
# where they won't dilute this pool's job.
#
# SECOND WRITING RULE, on variety: "just start" is the easiest motivational line to write and
# the pool drifts towards it if nobody is watching. It was once ten of eighteen lines, which
# made a big pool feel like a small one. Starting is one angle among many, so it is capped at
# the handful below and the rest of the pool covers what starting doesn't: continuing through
# the boring middle, coming back after a lapse, finishing, reps over talent, not measuring
# yourself against people whose practice you never saw, and saying the thing out loud. Before
# adding a line, check which of those it is, and whether the pool already has three of them.
#
# THIRD WRITING RULE, on the wry group: it is allowed to be funny, but it still has to push.
# The joke is on the situation, never on the reader, and a line that only gets a laugh belongs
# in wellbeing.txt. "Future you is not going to be more in the mood" earns its place because
# it is an argument for starting; a line that just makes light of procrastinating is not.

# Starting.
Start badly. A bad start beats a perfect plan you never opened.
The first five minutes are the whole fight. After that it carries itself.
Don't wait to feel like it. Feeling like it turns up after you start.
Nobody feels ready. They start anyway and work it out while moving.
Make the first step so small it's embarrassing, then take it.
Two minutes. Set a timer. Go.
Stop researching it. Go and do it badly.

# Avoidance.
Do the thing you've been walking past all week.
Whatever you're avoiding is smaller than your dread of it.
Close the tab that's quietly eating your afternoon.

# Momentum.
Momentum is a decision, not a mood.
You're one decision away from a completely different afternoon.

# Finishing.
Finish one thing today. One, all the way to done.
Half-done today beats flawless never.
Call it done at good enough. The last ten percent is where things go to die.

# The long game.
Give your future self something to work with.
Pick the thing that pays off in a year and do ten minutes of it.
Hard now or hard later. Only one of them gets easier.

# Coming back after a gap.
You don't have to start over. You have to start again, which is easier.
Missed a week? The streak was never the point. Go again today.
The day isn't ruined. There are hours left in it.

# The boring middle.
The middle is boring. That isn't a sign you're doing it wrong.
Boring and consistent wins. It just doesn't make a good story.
Do it on a day it doesn't feel worth doing. Those are the ones that count.

# Reps, not talent.
Everyone good at this was once visibly bad at it.
Talent is someone else's practice that you didn't watch.
You're not behind on ability. You're behind on reps.

# Comparison.
Run your own race. You can't see how far anyone else has come.
Compare yourself to last year's you. It's the only honest scoreboard.

# Saying the thing.
Send the message you keep rewriting in your head.
Ask for the thing. The worst case is the situation you're already in.
Say the honest sentence. It's shorter than the silence.

# Keeping your own word.
Do what you said you'd do. That's how you learn to trust yourself.
Keep one promise to yourself today. Any size counts.

# Attention and the body.
Stand up. Some of what feels like low motivation is just sitting still.
Guard one hour today like it belongs to someone you'd never let down.
One thing at a time, properly. Two at once is neither.
You have handled worse than this. Check your own record.

# Wry. Still pushes, just with a raised eyebrow.
Future you is not going to be more in the mood. Sorry.
The task is nine minutes long. You've spent twenty avoiding it.
Nobody is coming to do it for you. That was the plan and it fell through.
You've rehearsed this in your head enough times to be off book. Go.
Your list isn't going to read itself. That part, annoyingly, is yours.
