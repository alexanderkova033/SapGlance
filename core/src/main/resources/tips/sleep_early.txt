It's the middle of the night. If you can, put the phone down and get some rest.
Getting up when sleep won't come is stimulus control. The bed stays a cue for sleep.
Give your mind one specific thing to picture. That beat trying to clear it in a trial.
Turning to check the clock predicts a longer wait, not a shorter one.
Waking in the night is normal. Time awake in bed rises with age in healthy sleepers.
A bad night usually measures better than it feels. People underestimate what they slept.
If you get up, keep the light low. Ordinary room light is enough to move a body clock.
Alcohol's second half is now. This is when it fragments sleep and cuts REM short.
Caffeine's half-life runs from about 1.5 to 9.5 hours depending on the person.
Heat disturbs sleep more than cold does, once you have bedding you can adjust.
The wait to fall asleep feels far longer than it measures. That is normal too.
If you have been awake a while, get up. Twenty minutes is the usual guide.
Most people wake several times a night and remember none of it.
Lengthen the out-breath. It is the one lever that still works lying down in the dark.
