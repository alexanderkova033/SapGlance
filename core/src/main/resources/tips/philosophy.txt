# GROUP: PHILOSOPHY (TipKind.PHILOSOPHY) — reflection and perspective.
#
# Mostly QUOTATIONS, each carrying the public-domain text it comes from so the attribution is
# checkable rather than taken on trust. A handful of ORIGINAL reflections at the end carry no
# source and claim none.
#
# WRITING RULE FOR THIS POOL: only quote what can be pinned to a specific chapter, letter, or
# section in a public-domain edition, and quote that edition's actual wording. Popular
# philosophy quotes are misattributed constantly: the thousand-miles line gets pinned on
# Confucius, "we are what we repeatedly do" is Will Durant summarising Aristotle rather than
# Aristotle, and "he who has a why to live can bear almost any how" is Viktor Frankl's
# paraphrase, not Nietzsche's sentence. Nietzsche's actual line is the one below. Two more
# worth knowing, because they read as if they belong here and do not: "the greatest weapon
# against stress is our ability to choose one thought over another" is not William James, and
# "be yourself in a world constantly trying to make you something else" is not Emerson. Every
# line below was checked against the linked edition's text, not against a quotations site.
#
# SECOND WRITING RULE, on variety: this pool was once entirely Stoic plus a short tail (four
# Marcus, three Epictetus, three Seneca), which is a school, not a subject. Stoicism keeps the
# largest share because it suits the format, but the pool now also carries Confucian, Buddhist,
# Taoist, presocratic, rationalist, transcendentalist, essayist and nineteenth-century voices,
# and the ratio is worth defending: before adding another Roman, check whether a tradition
# below has only one line in it.
#
# THIRD WRITING RULE, on length: a quotation has to survive a small widget, so prefer the
# sentence that is already short in the source over a long one trimmed with an ellipsis.
# Cutting at a comma or a colon the original already has (as with the Dhammapada line below)
# is fine; cutting mid-clause and hoping nobody checks is how a quotation becomes a paraphrase.
#
# OPEN CRITICISM OF THIS POOL, 2026-07-28, not yet acted on: it is conventional. Every rule
# above is about being *accurate*, and the pool clears that bar; none of them is about being
# worth stopping on, and it does not clear that one. What is here is the philosophy everyone
# has already met, in the register of a poster. The suspected cause is selecting for
# quotability — a line short enough for a widget and instantly legible is usually a line that
# has been quoted to death. Prefer passages that need a beat to land, and ideas that cut
# against the reader rather than reassure them. See STATUS.md "the tone pools are too soft".
#
# Format is inline: "Tip text" alone, or "Tip text<TAB>Label<TAB>URL" for a quotation.
