# General micro-tips shown across morning/afternoon/evening, one per line.
# Each line has a matching line in general_sources.txt carrying 2+ citations.
Many eye doctors recommend the 20-20-20 rule: every 20 minutes, look 20 feet away for 20 seconds.
Blink fully, not just often. Incomplete blinks during screen use are linked to dry, tired eyes.
Stand up and give your legs a proper stretch before you sit back down.
Uncross your legs and roll your ankles right now.
Take 3 slow, deep breaths. Slow breathing shifts you toward calm within about a minute.
Match your screen brightness to the room to reduce eye strain.
A quick 5-minute walk resets focus better than pushing through does.
Screen-free breaks count twice: your eyes and your attention both get a rest.
Keep water within arm's reach. You drink more when reaching for it is free.
Roll your shoulders slowly backward five times and let them drop.
Unclench your jaw. Most people hold tension there without ever noticing.
Check your posture: ears, shoulders, and hips roughly stacked.
Glare is a common and easily missed cause of eye strain. Tilt the screen away.
Wiggle your fingers and stretch your wrists if you've been typing a while.
Breaks work because your brain stops tuning the task out, not because you rest.
Breathing out for longer than you breathe in is what triggers the calming response.
Adjust your chair so your feet rest flat on the floor.
One quiet minute with no screen and no sound is enough to feel a difference.
Break at a natural pause rather than mid-thought. It costs less to pick back up.
Loosen your grip on the mouse. You're holding it tighter than the job needs.
Two minutes of walking every half hour blunts the effect of a long sit.
Position your screen at eye level and an arm's length away to ease strain.
Let your chair's backrest support your lower back instead of leaning forward.
Mild dehydration dents focus and mood well before you actually feel thirsty.
Tip one ear toward your shoulder and hold ten seconds. Then the other side.
Rest your forearms on the desk or armrest instead of holding them up.
If a screen feels too close, increasing text size beats leaning in.
Keep your wrists straight while typing. Bent wrists raise carpal tunnel pressure.
Your best posture is your next one. Changing position beats holding a perfect one.
A cluttered desk quietly competes for your attention. A quick tidy pays for itself.
Long sitting is linked to worse health outcomes, even if you exercise regularly.
Switching between tasks has a real cost. Finishing one thing first is usually faster.
A glance at a notification costs far more focus than the glance itself takes.
A view of greenery, even through a window, helps you recover from mental fatigue.
Stuffy, high-CO2 rooms are linked to slower decisions. Cracking a window helps.
Stress often shows up first as tension in the jaw, neck, and shoulders.
Sustained poor posture is linked to more fatigue over the day, not just discomfort.
Media multitasking is linked to more stress, not less, even if it feels productive.
A brief walk, even indoors, boosts creative thinking more than sitting does.
Writing a task down frees the mental effort of holding on to it.
Daytime light exposure helps anchor your body clock too, not just mornings.
Two hours a week outdoors, in one go or spread across the week, tracks with better health.
Strong social ties predict how long people live about as well as giving up smoking does.
Doing something kind for someone else gives your own mood a small, reliable lift.
Fibre is the nutrient most people fall short on. The benefits show up around 25 to 30g a day.
Inhale, add a second short inhale on top, then a long exhale. Five minutes a day beat meditation for mood.
Long text sinks in better on paper than on screen, and the gap widens when you're rushed.
Blue-light glasses probably don't help eye strain. A Cochrane review of 17 trials found no benefit.
Overheard speech breaks concentration more than steady noise does, even at low volume.
Office task performance peaks near 21 to 22C. Warmer or cooler both measurably cost you.
